  • Evaluate welfare, wait times, housing affordability, and access to amenities across subpopulations
  • (Sequel) Changing allocation rules vs. increased supply

Applicants apply for BTO as a family unit (typically a married couple)

  • Income \( \leq \) S$144,000 a year (60th percentile)

Priority: Focus on “first-timers.”

  • 95% of apartments in desirable neighborhoods reserved for them

4. Model: Outline of Public Housing Market

  • New apartment supply exogenous;
  • Owner-occupiers sell or not;
  • Applicants enter one lottery, apply to clearinghouse, wait, or exit

4.1. Dynamic equilibrium model of housing choice

Every period, new housing developments arrive exogenously.

Owner-occupiers

  • Infinite horizon, two-period-ahead foresight through 2023
    • Know price path next period; form beliefs over values period after
  • Given the prices they face, either
    1. Sell off their apartment (terminal); or
    2. Continue living in it
  • Key trade-off: Sell today vs. sell at higher price tomorrow

4.2. Applicants

  • Infinite horizon, two-period-ahead foresight
    • Differ by income, family structure, …
    • Know projects arriving next period; form beliefs over values after
  • Given prefs, prices and odds of success over developments, either
    1. Apply for one project within the mechanism;
    2. Apply to one region in the clearinghouse;
    3. Buy a “used” apartment in some region; or
    4. Wait (live with family).
  • Key trade-off: Success odds vs. desirability

Markets clear via lottery odds (1\(^{\circ} \)) and prices (2\(^{\circ} \))

5. Model: Details

(FAST FORWARD: ESTIMATION) (FAST FORWARD: COUNTERFACTUALS)

5.1. Model — Beliefs

Owner-occupiers and applicants have limited foresight. Beliefs @ \( t \):

t \( \hat V \)? \(\widehat{\text{Applicant mix}}\)? Know project mix? \( \widehat{P(S)} \)?
\( t+2 \) \( V^t_{t+2} = V^t\) \( \mu_t \) No \( P^t_{t+2}(S) \)
\( t+1 \) \( V^t_{t+1} \) \( \mu_t \) Yes \( P_{t+1}(S) \)
\( t \) \( V^t_t \) \( \mu_t \) Yes \( P_t(S) \)

Assume beliefs are updated as \[ V^t = \frac{V^t_t + \delta V^t_{t+1}}{1+\delta} \] (and similarly for resale prices)

5.2. Used apartments: Notation

Every quarter \( t \): owner-occupier \( i \) decides whether to sell off her apartment

  • Action 1/0: To sell/not
  • \( a \): type (location) of the used apartment
  • \( P_t(S; a) \): market price of apartments of type \( a \)
  • \( V^t_{it}[S; a, P(S; a)] \): Believed value of owning type \( a \) apartment
    • Abusing algebra, suppress dependence on price path \( P(S; a) \)

5.3. Used apartments: Indirect utilities

Every quarter \( t \): owner-occupier \( i \) decides whether to sell off her apartment

  • Indirect utility from selling is \[ w_{it}(1; a) = \rho P_t(S; a) + \xi_{t}(1, S; a) + \epsilon_{it}(1, S; a) \]
  • Indirect utility from staying is \[ w_{it}(0; a) = \delta(S) \mathbb{E}[V^t_{it+1}(S; a)] + \xi_{t}(0, S; a) + \epsilon_{it}(0, S; a) \]

5.4. Used apartment: Choice probabilities and Supply

  • Conditional choice probability of type \( a \) to sell at time \( t \): \[ p_{t}(1; a, P(S)) = \frac{\exp[w_t(1; a, P(S))]}{\exp[w_t(0; a, P(S))] + \exp[w_t(1; a, P(S))]} \]
  • In region \( l \), supply of secondary market housing at time \( t\) is \[ \mathbb{S}_t( l, P(S)) = \int_{a \in l} p_{t}(1; a, P(S)) \, d\lambda_t(a), \] where \( \lambda_t(a) \) is the housing stock of type \( a \) apartments at time \( t \).

5.5. Model — Demand (Preferences)

Specify model backwards.

  • Applicant \( i \): 2-tuple \(\omega_i = (\zeta_i, s_i) \), (obs. char., unobs. type.)
  • BTO project \( j \): 4-tuple \((l_{jt}, x_{jt}, P_{jt}, \xi_{jt}) \)
    • (region, obs. char., price vector by type, unobs. amenities by type)
    • \( L_{jt} \): 5-vector of regions, with entries \( \mathbb{1}\{ l_{jt} = l \}\)
    • Probability of success at \( j \): \( r_{jt}(\omega) \)
  • Clearinghouse option \((C, l)\): region \( l \), price vector \( P_t(C, l; \omega) \)
    • Probability of success: \( r_{t}(C, l; \omega) \)
  • Exit option \((E, l)\): region \( l \), price vector \( P_t(E, l; \omega) \)

5.6. Flow payoffs (1)

If applicant \( i \) of type \( \omega \) remains unmatched, her flow utility is \[ u_t(0; \omega) = \xi_t(W; \omega) + \delta V^t_{t+1}(\omega). \]

Else her terminal utilities from success are:

  • Apartment in BTO project \( j \): \[ u_t(j; \omega) = L_{jt}'\color{blue}{\phi(\omega)} + x_{jt}'\color{blue}{\beta(\omega)} - \color{blue}{\alpha(\omega)} P_{jt}(\omega) + \xi_{t}(j; \omega) \]
    • \( \phi(\omega) \): Governs preference for project \( j \) being in \( i \)’s preferred region(s)
    • \( \beta(\omega) \): Governs preferences over other observables of project \( j \)
    • \( \alpha(\omega) \): Governs preferences over apartment price

5.7. Flow payoffs (2)

  • Apartment in BTO project \( j \): \[ u_t(j; \omega) = L_{jt}'\phi(\omega) + x_{jt}'\beta(\omega) - \alpha(\omega) P_{jt}(\omega) + \xi_{t}(j; \omega) \]
  • Apartment on clearinghouse in region \( l \): \[ u_t(C, l; \omega) = L(C, l)'\phi(\omega) - \alpha(\omega) P_{t}(C, l; \omega) + \xi_{t}(C, l; \omega) \]
  • Apartment on secondary market in region \( l \): \[ u_t(E, l; \omega) = L(E, l)'\phi(\omega) - \alpha(\omega) P_t(E, l; \omega) \]

5.8. Choice-specific value functions

  • BTO apartment \( j \): \[ v_{it}(j; \omega) = r_{t}(j; \omega) u_t(j; \omega) + [1-r_{t}(j; \omega)] u_t(0; \omega) + \epsilon_{it}(j; \omega) \]
  • Clearinghouse, region \( l \): \[ v_{it}(C, l; \omega) = r_{t}(C, l; \omega) u_t(C, l; \omega) + [1-r_{t}(C, l; \omega)] u_t(0; \omega) + \epsilon_{it}(C, l; \omega) \]
  • Apartment on secondary market, region \( l \): \[ v_{it}(E, l; \omega) = u_t(E, l; \omega) + \epsilon_{it}(E, l; \omega) \]
  • Wait one period: \[ v_{it}(W; \omega) = u_t(0; \omega) + \epsilon_{it}(W; \omega). \]

5.9. Household decision problem

  • If unmatched, decides optimally: \[ V^t_t(\omega) = \mathbb{E}\left\{\max_{d \in \mathcal{J}_t \cup \mathcal{C}_t \cup \mathcal{E}_t \cup \{ W\}} \mathbb{E}_t[v_{it}(d; \omega)] \right\} \]
  • Possible actions:
    • \( d \in \mathcal{J}_t \): apply to BTO project \( d \)
    • \( d \in \mathcal{C}_t \): apply to clearinghouse, region \( d \)
    • \( d \in \mathcal{E}_t \): take used apartment, region \( d \)
    • \( d = W \): wait one period

5.10. Market shares

  • Type-specific market share of BTO project \( j \) at time \( t \): \[ p_{t}(j; \omega) = \frac{\exp(\bar v_{t}(j; \omega))}{\sum_{l \in \mathcal{L}}[\exp(\bar v_{t}(C, l; \omega)) + \exp(\bar v_{t}(E, l; \omega))] + \exp(\bar v_{t}(W; \omega)) + \sum_{k \in \mathcal{J}_t} \exp(\bar v_{t}(k; \omega))} \]

5.11. Model — Equilibrium

In equilibrium,

  1. [Applicants apply/wait/exit optimally]
  2. [Owner-occupiers sell/remain optimally]
  3. [Applicant and owner-occupier beliefs are consistent]
  4. [Success probabilities in the mechanism are consistent]
  5. [Sequence of secondary market prices sets demand equal to supply]

7. Estimation: Details

(FAST FORWARD: DEMAND ESTIMATION)

7.1. Demand estimation: Outline

Demand estimation combines techniques from labor and IO.

  1. Perform data-driven estimation of latent demand types (k-means; e.g., Bonhomme, Lamadon, and Manresa 2019; 2022). Obtain five applicant “types”
  2. Form conditional choice probabilities by applicant type
  3. Recover determinants of applicant utility
    1. (Offline) Use RD across subsidy cutoffs to get price elasticity of demand
    2. Use finite dependence (e.g., Arcidiacono and Miller 2011) to “cancel” forward-looking part of utility
    3. Recover weights on amenities using IV (Hotz and Miller 1993; Berry 1994; Kalouptsidi, Scott, and Souza-Rodrigues 2021)

7.2. Demand estimation: (1) Data-driven estimation of latent demand types

Challenge
Rich data on applicant demographics and application history. “Let data speak” to which applicants are similar.
Solution
Cluster via k-means (Lloyd 1982).
  • Characteristics: Household income, age of main applicant, maximal number of children in sample, modal region of application, length of time from first to last application, discrete characteristic 1 (4 values), discrete characteristic 2 (2 values)
  • Use elbow method to assess number of clusters (\(k^{*} = 6 \)).

7.3. Demand estimation: (2) Estimating preferences type-by-type

Once types/CCPs are recovered, applicant preferences are obtained by standard IV techniques. Fix a type \( \omega \) and a time period \( t \); suppress subscripts.

  • Take two BTO projects \( j, k \in \mathcal{J}_{t} \).
  • Construct the object \[ \frac{1}{1 - r(j)} v(j) = \frac{r(j)}{1-r(j)} u(j) + u(0) \]
    • Continuation value only appears in \( u(0) \)
  • Take differences between \( j \) and \( k \): \[ \frac{1}{1 - r(j)} \ln p(j) - \frac{1}{1 - r(k)} \ln p(k) = \frac{r(j)}{1-r(j)} u(j) - \frac{r(k)}{1-r(k)} u(k) \]
  • Does not depend on continuation value!

(FAST FORWARD: COUNTERFACTUALS)

7.4. Demand estimation: Recover preferences

\[ \frac{1}{1 - r(j)} \ln p(j) - \frac{1}{1 - r(k)} \ln p(k) = \frac{r(j)}{1-r(j)} u(j) - \frac{r(k)}{1-r(k)} u(k) \]

Estimate parameters by IV regression (Berry 1994)

  • Variation: Between projects within period
  • Issue 1: Observables explain well prices of new builds
    • Use RD across subsidy cutoffs to get price elasticity of demand \( \hat \alpha(\omega) \)
  • Issue 2: Endogeneity between \( r(j) \) and \( \xi(j) \)
    • Use differentiation IV (Gandhi and Houde 2020)
  • Obtain: \( \hat \phi(\omega), \hat \beta(\omega) \)
  • SEs: Bayesian bootstrap over applicants (Rubin 1981)
    1. In each replication, draw weights for each applicant
    2. Use weighted k-means to redraw clusters
    3. Use weights in IV regression

7.5. Demand estimation: Recover structural residuals

  • Exiting is terminal. For any region \( l \), tomorrow’s value: \[ \color{blue}{\hat V'} = \gamma_{\text{Euler}} + L(E, l)^T \hat \phi - \hat\alpha P'(E, l) - \ln p'(E, l) \]
  • Compare waiting and exiting today: \[ \ln p(W) - \ln p(E, l) = \color{blue}{\xi(W)} + \delta \hat V' - L(E, l)^T \hat \phi + \hat \alpha P(E, l) \]
  • Solve for \( \hat \xi(W) \).
  • Finally, compare applying to \( j \) and exiting: \[ \ln p(j) - \ln p(E, l) = \text{known stuff} + r(j) \color{blue}{\xi(j)} + (1-r(j)) \left[ \hat \xi(W) + \delta \hat{V}' \right] \]
  • Solve for \( \hat \xi(j) \).
