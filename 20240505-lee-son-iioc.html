2. This paper: School choice with limited consideration

Applicants can only consider a limited number of schools and may have mistaken beliefs about admission chances.

  • Data: 2016-2017, NYC HS application records + demographics
  • Mechanism: Student-proposing DA, max 12 programs

Identification

  • Consideration: Distance to school + Position in NYC HS directory
  • Preferences: Application rank-ordered lists
  • Beliefs: “Deviation” of actual cutoffs and scores from student-predicted analogs (by iid noise + f(program rank in student’s list))

5. My thoughts

The authors tackle an important topic, and have to overcome difficult identification challenges regarding beliefs and consideration.

Where I’d push: How sure are we of the “ground truth”?

  • Good: The paper’s message concurs with that of Allende et al (2023).
  • Tricky: Consideration rests on school position on the NYC HS directory.
    • Marketing? Probaby not Stuyvesant, but other schools
  • How robust are the paper’s findings to different specifications of beliefs/consideration?
  • Could the authors interview participants in the mechanism?
    • Ideally: Survey participants (e.g., Kapor, Neilson, and Zimmerman 2020) to better ascertain the “ground truth”.

6. My other thoughts

  • How about applicants who have older siblings who participated in the school choice process? Maybe their beliefs are less biased or they consider more schools.
  • What happens on the aftermarket? Maybe matching inefficiencies are “not so bad” if an aftermarket allows them to be corrected.

Other nitpicks

  • Figure 2 (Prob. & Latent Values): More informative label + Binscatter?
  • Welfare in terms of distance to school?
